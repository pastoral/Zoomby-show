# Zoomby storytelling rules (any topic)

## Format
A countdown of **8–10 surprising, funny and human facts** about one subject. It works for a person, an animal, a place, a brand, a sport or a historical event. The best fact goes last (#1). Total length is about 160–190 words, 4–6 s per scene, and 35–65 s overall.

## Hook (0–3 s)
- **Title line (default):** "Ten weird things you didn't know about [SUBJECT FULL NAME]."
- **Superlative question:** "What if the [superlative] [thing] on the planet has weird secrets that make it even more insane?"
- **Shocking fact first:** start with the #1 fact's set-up, then "…and that's not even the weirdest thing."

The name lands on a big close-up. The first scene starts at about 2.6–3.1 s.

## Scene unit = set-up → punchline
- **Set-up:** one sentence of 8–18 words with a **hard detail**: a number, a named person, place or brand, or a time anchor ("At five years old…", "This man…", "As a kid…").
- **Punchline:** 2–8 words. Rotate the devices and never use the same one twice in a row:

| Device | Pattern | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Contrast flip | That's not X, that's Y. | "That's not a diet, that's a crime scene." |
| Parallel pair | [Verb] like A. [Verb] like B. | "Sleep like a serial killer. Perform like a god." |
| Absurd simile | …like [mundane modern thing]. | "…like he's waiting for his Uber." |
| Triple negation | Not X. Not Y. [Surprise]. | "Not his agent. Not his coach. His grandma." |
| Fragment echo | Repeat the key word. | "Not one. Five." · "Pandas." |
| Slang verdict | [Slang label]. | "Absolute freak behavior." · "Bro was already cheating physics." |
| Victim POV | Show the other side. | "The goalkeeper just wanted to go home." |
| Ironic contrast | Elite win + petty loss. | "Wins the Champions League, but loses to salad dressing." |

## Flow
- **Rapid-fire:** no connectors; the cut and badge carry the transition.
- **Connectors** (use each one at most once): First off · Next · And that's not all · But it gets weirder · But here's the wildest part · Oh, and · And on top of that · One more thing · Then there's this · Finally · And the wildest?

## Tone
An **affectionate roast**: amazed and teasing at the same time. Use Gen-Z slang only in punchlines (bro, my guy, ghost mode, freak behavior, rage-baiting, slayed it, … energy) and keep the set-ups plain. Never mock appearance, race, religion, disability, family hardship or tragedy. Sentences average 7–9 words, with 4–10 fragments per video.

## Endings (no spoken CTA)

| Style | Example |
|---|---|
| Cliffhanger | "But have you ever heard about the…" (cut mid-line) |
| Deadpan | "Yep, himself." |
| Simile | "…walks away like a monk." |
| Legend | "…and he's just getting started." |
| Gag | censored-swear bubble |

## Facts
- Mix 5–7 categories: body or diet, sleep or rituals, family, childhood, money or fame, hobbies or fears, records.
- Verify every fact against at least one reliable source. Use "reportedly" for anything unconfirmed, and drop anything defamatory or medical speculation.
- Every number should look good as a caption.

## Topic suggestions (Stage 1)
When the user asks for ideas, offer **5 topics** that each:
- centre on **one famous subject** (a person, an animal, a place, a brand, a team or an event) that people already know by name;
- have **10+ verifiable odd, funny or human public facts**, enough for 8–10 scenes with spares;
- give **visual gags**: literal props and photoreal locations the cartoon can act in (a feast hall, a hotel bed full of balls, a prison yard pitch…);
- can be **roasted affectionately**, with no tragedy-driven facts, legal accusations, or minors as the butt of the joke.

Before suggesting, read `VIDEOS_BASE/_ideas/*.md` and the existing video folder names. Don't repeat a made topic; you may re-offer unpicked ones and label them "(from <date> list)". Mix it up: at most 3 from the same subject category unless the user asked for one category.

Format (also saved to `_ideas/YYYY-MM-DD-topics.md`):
```
1. "10 weird things you didn't know about <SUBJECT>"
   Angle: <one-line roast angle>
   Facts: <fact> · <fact> · <fact>   (first-pass; verified in Stage 2)
   Gags: <location/prop> · <location/prop> · <location/prop>
   Ending idea: <style + line>
```
Mark facts you are not sure of as "reportedly" already in the list.

## Notes from the 6 reference episodes
- Hooks often frame the subject as a superlative question ("What if the deadliest striker on the planet has weird secrets that make him even more insane?") over a hero shot running at the camera.
- One fact = 4.5–6 s of voice, carried by 3–4 visual beats; the punchline lands on the last shot of the fact (usually a face close-up or a sight gag).
- The last fact can end on a "Like & Subscribe" end card or a deadpan line; no spoken call to action.
